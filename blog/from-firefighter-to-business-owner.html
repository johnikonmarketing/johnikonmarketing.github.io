From Firefighter to Business Owner: What Life-Safety Work Teaches About Running a Company

John Dillon

I spent 14 years as a firefighter/EMT with Frederick County Fire and Rescue in Maryland before I ever ran a business. When people hear that background and then find out I later owned an electrical contracting company, the question I get most is: what actually carries over?

More than you'd think. Not the adrenaline — the discipline.

Checklists aren't optional

Fire and rescue work runs on checklists, pre-plans, and standard operating procedures, because the moment you improvise on a structural fire or a technical rescue is the moment something goes wrong. That same mindset carried directly into running a commercial electrical business: scope the job before you start, confirm what's actually behind the wall before you cut into it, and don't skip steps because you're in a hurry.

Accountability isn't negotiable

On a fire ground, every person on scene is accountable for their assignment, and command needs to know exactly where everyone is at all times. Running a business with subcontractors and crews on multiple job sites isn't life-or-death in the same way, but the underlying principle is identical: you need to know who's doing what, where, and whether it's actually getting done — not assume it is.

Staying calm when something goes sideways

Every commercial project eventually hits a surprise — a panel that doesn't match the as-built drawings, a schedule that gets compressed by a general contractor, a client who needs an answer immediately. Years of responding to actual emergencies changes how you react to a business "emergency." You slow down, assess, and fix the actual problem instead of panicking about it.

Why this matters to clients

When I founded IKON Electric, that background is part of what shaped how the company operated — reliable, code-compliant, and communicative with clients from national retail brands to automotive dealership groups. It's also, I'd guess, part of why the business built a 5.0-star rating across 33 Google reviews and a 100% recommend rate on Facebook: people notice when a contractor treats safety and follow-through as non-negotiable rather than optional.
