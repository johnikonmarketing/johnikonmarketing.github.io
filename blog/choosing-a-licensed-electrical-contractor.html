Choosing a Licensed Electrical Contractor: What Commercial Property Managers Should Look For

John Dillon

Commercial property managers deal with electrical contractors regularly — tenant build-outs, panel and lighting upgrades, service calls, code-compliance repairs. The gap between a contractor who makes that process easy and one who creates ongoing headaches usually comes down to a few specific things.

Licensing and Master Electrician oversight

Proper licensing isn't just a formality — a Master Electrician's involvement means someone with deep, tested knowledge of code and commercial installation standards is accountable for the work. Ask directly who on the team holds that license and how involved they actually are in the projects, not just the paperwork.

Willingness to explain, not just quote

A contractor who can walk a property manager through what's actually being done and why — not just hand over a number — is one who understands the work well enough to stand behind it. If a contractor can't explain a scope of work in plain terms, that's worth noticing.

Track record with similar properties

Experience across national retail brands, automotive dealership groups, and general contractors isn't just a resume line — it means a contractor has already worked through the specific scheduling pressure, code requirements, and coordination challenges that come with those property types. That history shows up in how smoothly the next job goes.

Reviews that hold up under scrutiny

Reviews matter, but consistency matters more than volume — a 5.0-star rating across a real, verifiable set of reviews on Google and a 100% recommend rate on Facebook says more than a handful of five-star ratings with no detail behind them. It's worth actually reading a few, not just checking the star count.

Responsiveness before you're a client

How a contractor communicates before they've been hired is usually a preview of how they'll communicate once they have the job. Slow, vague responses during the bidding process rarely improve once the contract is signed.
