Working With General Contractors: What Makes a Retail Build-Out Succeed

John Dillon

Tenant build-outs for national retail brands run on some of the tightest timelines in commercial construction — a store often has a hard opening date tied to marketing, leasing, and corporate rollout schedules that won't move for a subcontractor's convenience. Working electrical scope on build-outs for tenants like Puma, Lids, Victoria's Secret, Under Armour, and Nike at Tanger Outlets taught me a lot about what actually makes a GC relationship work under that kind of pressure.

Answer the phone before it becomes a problem

General contractors managing a multi-trade build-out are juggling a dozen moving pieces. The subcontractors they keep calling back are the ones who respond quickly and give a straight answer — "yes, on schedule," "no, here's why, and here's the fix" — rather than the ones who go quiet when something's off.

Show up ready to solve problems, not just report them

Every build-out hits something unexpected — a wall that isn't where the drawings say it is, a panel location that conflicts with fixture placement, a delivery that's a day behind. A GC doesn't need a subcontractor who just flags the problem; they need one who shows up with two or three ways to fix it and a recommendation.

Repeat relationships are earned, not assumed

Chrysler Dodge Plymouth of Franklin was IKON Electric's first commercial client, and that relationship led to multiple projects over time — the same pattern played out with Mercedes-Benz of Franklin, Nissan Franklin, and Toyota Franklin. General contractors and property groups don't keep calling a contractor back because of a single good bid; they call back because the work was done right the first time and the communication was easy the whole way through.

The trades that coordinate well finish well

On a multi-trade build-out, electrical work touches almost every other trade — framing, HVAC, fire protection, fixtures, signage. Contractors who coordinate proactively with the other trades on site, instead of working in isolation and hoping it lines up, are the ones whose sections of the job pass inspection the first time.
